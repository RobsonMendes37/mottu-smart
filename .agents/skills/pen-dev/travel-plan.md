# Travel planning

Use the [whiteboard guide](./whiteboard.md) to turn a trip idea into a browsable plan.

Use the destination, dates, departure city, travelers, budget, and interests already supplied. Ask for missing details when they affect the choices, while beginning useful destination research. If the user is still exploring, build an inspiration board rather than inventing fares or availability.

## Build the board

- Keep shortlisted transport, stays, and activities open together.
- For a road trip, put the route map beside the itinerary and arrange the legs left to right in travel order. Place each stop's evidence beside the decision it supports. For a city stay, group by neighborhood or day in the same way.
- For a Google Maps route, wait for directions to load, then collapse the open panel with the chevron on its edge. Recheck after reloading. At the browser node’s final size, adjust the zoom if needed so both endpoints and the whole route are visible. Keep the directions link beside the map.
- Identify the constraint most likely to break the plan, such as an arrival after the last train. Check one workable fallback and show how much time or money it adds for the whole party. Label any unverified difference as an estimate.
- Assemble a relaxed, geographically sensible itinerary when enough details are known. Account for opening days, arrival and departure times, meals, transfers, and rest.
- Add a simple budget if useful, distinguishing observed prices, estimates, and unknown costs.

Compare prices for the same dates and party size. Distinguish nightly from total stay costs, include known fees, and make important baggage or cancellation terms visible. Check current venue information where timing matters; a property's advertised starting price isn't confirmed availability.

Finish with the choices still open and the most useful trade-offs. Keep the source browsers available so the user can continue planning. Leave bookings, payments, and personal-detail submissions to the user.
