# Website sitemap

Use the [whiteboard guide](./whiteboard.md) to map an existing website's structure.

Start with the supplied URL. Use its sitemap when available, following sitemap indexes, then explore navigation and crawl public links to fill gaps. Stay within the requested site or section, deduplicate URLs without merging distinct pages, and respect crawl restrictions. Keep external destinations separate.

## Build the board

- By default, draw a sitemap tree directly below the answer. Start at the homepage (or the requested section), branch into sections, and place pages under their primary navigation parent. Use editable page labels and connecting lines, or an indented outline for a compact tree.
- Show a shared page once and mark other routes to it as cross-links. For a large site, summarize dense branches with page counts and keep their URLs in the inventory.
- Show one route a visitor cares about, such as Home → Pricing → Signup. Follow the links and record the actual sequence and destinations. Stop at an access or submission boundary and mark the last verified step.
- Label groupings inferred from URL paths separately from navigation you followed. Keep the homepage, section pages, and useful examples open beside their findings.
- Keep a linked inventory of discovered URLs. For a small site, open every page; for a large site, keep representative browsers and preserve the full inventory in canvas tables or a linked file.

Show how much was discovered and how much was actually inspected. If access restrictions or a crawl limit leave work unfinished, make that visible and retain the remaining URLs for follow-up. Describe the result as the pages discovered within scope, rather than claiming every possible page was found.
